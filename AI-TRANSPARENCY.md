## How the work was split

### Done by AI (large share)
- Almost all **Java source** (`dev.bodycam.*`) — camera state, temporal effects,
  springs, breathing, water, post-FX, config, GUI, mixins and compat bridges.
- **GLSL shaders** (`post.fsh`, `dofblur.fsh`, JSON shader definitions).
- **Build files** (`build.gradle`, `settings.gradle`, wrapper config).
- **Assets**: the SVG logo and derived `icon.png`, texture processing
  (resizing/compressing the lens-dirt overlay), the WAV → OGG conversion pipeline.
- **Documentation**: README, Modrinth description, changelog, dev notes, wiki.

### Done by the human author
- The overall **vision and requirements** (what a bodycam mod should feel like).
- **Direction**: every session was driven by explicit human requests and priorities.
- **Testing in-game**: builds were installed, played and verified; bugs and
  feel problems were reported back ("the world is corrupted", "aiming is
  mirrored", "make it more extreme", …).
- **Decisions and review**: presets, difficulty/feel tuning, what ships and what
  does not (e.g. a cancelled rainbow-ring fix was the author's call).
- **Hand-written / hand-modified code**: the author wrote some of the code from
  scratch and **directly modified other AI-generated code** by hand — so the
  codebase is a mix, not a pure AI output.
- Assets that are 100 % human (e.g. the dirty-lens source overlay, gameplay GIFs).

### Process in one line
> human asks → AI implements → human plays and judges → AI fixes → repeat.

## Why disclose

Generative AI played a **significant role** in producing this project, so by
[Modrinth's Content Rules](https://modrinth.com/rules) and Modrinth's AI
disclosure policy it must be flagged. We would consider AI a co-author — so it is.

Nothing here is hidden: this file, the README badge/section, the changelog
(including session logs) and the Modrinth listing all say the same thing.
